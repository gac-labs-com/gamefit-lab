Gaming posture: how to set up your desk and chair

Sep 28, 2026 · 6 min read · No products, no affiliate links

Long sessions are hard on your body, and the usual culprit is not one terrible position but staying in any position for too long. A decent setup helps. Moving regularly helps more.

Heads up: this is general information, not medical advice. If you have pain that is severe, getting worse, or travels down an arm or leg, see a qualified professional.

The goal: a neutral, supported position

You are aiming for a position where no joint is stretched or twisted for long. For most people that looks like this. OSHA notes there is no single correct posture that fits everyone, so treat it as a starting point:

Body partWhat to aim for
FeetFlat on the floor or a footrest, not dangling or tucked under you.
Knees and hipsKnees roughly at a right angle, with your thighs supported by the seat.
BackSupported by the chair, with the natural curve of your lower back filled in. A rolled towel works if your chair has no support.
ElbowsClose to your body, bent about 90 degrees, with forearms roughly level.
WristsStraight, not bent up or sideways, with your hands resting at the same height as your forearms.
ScreenAbout an arm's length away, with the top of the screen at or slightly below eye level.

Set up your desk in five minutes

  1. Start with the chair height. Set it so your feet are flat and your knees are about level with your hips.
  2. Move your keyboard and mouse close. If you have to reach forward, your shoulders and neck pay for it.
  3. Raise the screen if needed. A stack of books works. You should not be looking down or craning your neck up.
  4. Check your mouse arm. Rest the forearm on the desk or armrest so your shoulder can relax.
  5. Sit back. Let the chair take your weight instead of perching on the edge.

Playing on a couch, bed or floor

Console and handheld players often end up slumped. Some things that help: support your lower back with a pillow, bring the screen up to eye level, and rest your elbows against your sides or on pillows so your arms are not held up in the air. Lying flat on your stomach with your head propped up is hard on your neck, so keep those sessions short.

The best posture is your next posture

Even a perfect setup gets uncomfortable if you never move. Use the natural pauses in your games:

  • Between matches and in loading screens: stand up, roll your shoulders and shake out your hands.
  • About every 30 to 60 minutes: take a short walk or stretch for a minute or two.
  • Change position now and then, such as leaning back, sitting upright, or standing if you can.

Three easy stretches

  • Chest opener: clasp your hands behind your back and gently lift, or press your forearms against a doorframe and lean forward.
  • Neck: gently tilt your ear toward your shoulder and hold for a few breaths on each side. Never force it.
  • Wrist and forearm: extend one arm in front of you, palm up, and use the other hand to gently pull the fingers down. Switch sides.

Stretches should feel like mild tension, never sharp pain.

When to see a professional

  • Pain that does not ease after a few days of better habits.
  • Numbness, tingling or weakness in your hands or arms.
  • Pain that wakes you at night or spreads down an arm or leg.

FAQ

Do I need an expensive chair?

Not necessarily. Whether a chair fits you and lets you adjust height, back support and armrests matters more than its price or looks. Many of the fixes above cost nothing.

Is standing all day better than sitting?

Not on its own. Switching between positions and moving often is better than staying in any single one for hours.

Does posture matter for controllers?

Yes. Keep your elbows relaxed, your wrists straight and take breaks to shake out your hands.
