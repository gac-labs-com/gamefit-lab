Gaming eye strain: what actually helps

Sep 21, 2026 · 5 min read · No products, no affiliate links

If your eyes feel dry, tired or sore after a long session, you are not imagining it. Staring at a screen changes how we use our eyes, and gaming makes it more intense. Most of the fixes are free and take seconds.

Heads up: this is general information, not medical advice. Eye symptoms that are sudden, severe or persistent need a proper check from an eye professional.

Why gaming tires your eyes

  • Close, constant focus. Your eyes stay focused at one distance for a long time.
  • Less blinking. People tend to blink less when concentrating on a screen, which can leave eyes feeling dry and gritty. The American Academy of Ophthalmology (AAO) notes people blink about 5 to 7 times a minute at a computer, compared with about 15 normally.
  • Glare and contrast. A bright screen in a dark room, or reflections from a window, force your eyes to work harder.
  • Long sessions. The longer you go without a break, the more it adds up.

The 20-20-20 habit

A well-known rule of thumb: every 20 minutes, look at something about 20 feet (6 metres) away for at least 20 seconds. It gives your focusing muscles a break. In games, the easiest moments are loading screens, respawns and the time between matches.

Fix the setup

  1. Match brightness to the room. If the screen looks like a lamp, it is too bright for the room. If you cannot read it, it is too dim.
  2. Add some ambient light. A small light behind the screen reduces the harsh contrast of a dark room.
  3. Position the screen to avoid glare. Put it at right angles to windows if possible, rather than facing or backing onto one.
  4. Check the distance. About an arm's length for a monitor, a bit further for a TV.
  5. Keep text readable. If you squint at menus or subtitles, increase their size in the game settings.

Blink and hydrate

Make a point of blinking fully and deliberately now and then, especially during intense moments. Drink water through the session. If your eyes often feel dry, a pharmacist or eye professional can suggest options that suit you.

What about blue light?

Night modes and warmer screen colours are popular, and some people find them more comfortable in the evening. The AAO says there is no scientific evidence that blue light from screens damages your eyes, and it does not recommend special blue-light-blocking glasses for computer use. Breaks, a good setup and sensible session lengths are more reliable. Warm colours in the evening may also help you wind down for sleep, but they are not a cure for eye strain.

Plan your sessions

  • Take a real break every hour or two: stand up and look out of a window.
  • Avoid playing in the dark on a phone or handheld held close to your face.
  • Stop earlier on days when your eyes already feel tired.

When to see an eye professional

  • Blurred or double vision that does not clear after resting.
  • Frequent headaches, eye pain or sensitivity to light.
  • Redness or irritation that does not improve.
  • It has been a while since your last eye test. Regular exams are worth it, especially if you game or work on screens a lot.

FAQ

Can gaming damage my eyes permanently?

The AAO says eye strain does not cause permanent damage. If you notice changes in your vision, get them checked.

Do I need special glasses?

Not to get relief from eye strain. If you already wear glasses or contacts, make sure your prescription is up to date. An eye professional can tell you whether anything else would help.

Does a bigger screen help?

Not by itself. Distance, brightness, glare and breaks matter more than size.
